For a school ID card printing business, much of the work happens before the first card reaches the printer. Student details need to be collected, photographs need to be taken, and every image needs to match the correct record. Across multiple schools, these small tasks quickly become a large coordination effort.

A handwritten name must be typed again. A photograph number must be checked against a paper form. An incomplete record can hold up a batch that was otherwise ready to print.

**Penport ID Card Solution** brings this collection process into a digital workflow. Schools submit student details and live photographs through an Android application, and the information becomes available in the service provider's account for ID card preparation, printing and delivery.

## The work behind the traditional ID card process

School ID card service providers have traditionally handled collection through paper forms, school visits and spreadsheets.

1. **Distribute paper forms.** The provider sends printed forms to schools for student information.
2. **Collect handwritten details.** Parents, guardians or teachers fill in the forms.
3. **Visit the school for photography.** The provider photographs students individually using a digital camera.
4. **Record photograph numbers.** Each photograph's number is written on the corresponding student's form.
5. **Type the information into Excel.** Handwritten records are entered into a spreadsheet and matched with the photographs.
6. **Prepare, print and deliver the cards.** Once records and images are organized, production can begin.

Each step depends on the previous one being complete and accurate. When a provider serves several schools, there are more forms, more photographs and more opportunities for something to get missed.

## Where manual work creates delays and errors

### The same information is entered twice

Student details are first written on paper and then typed into Excel. That second entry takes time and introduces opportunities for spelling mistakes or incorrect details, particularly when handwriting is difficult to read.

### Photographs and records must be matched separately

A photo number connects an image to a paper form. If that number is missing or recorded incorrectly, a student's card may receive the wrong photograph. Finding the correct match adds another round of checking.

### Missing information can mean another school visit

An incomplete form or a missed photograph can require follow-ups and additional visits. Meanwhile, staff must keep track of which records are ready and which still need attention.

### Administrative work slows down production

Sorting forms, organizing photo folders and checking spreadsheets all compete with the work of preparing and printing cards. Corrections can also mean reprinting, adding to the workload and delaying delivery.

## How Penport ID Card Solution changes the workflow

Penport ID Card Solution is designed primarily for **School ID Card Service Providers and ID Card Printing Businesses**.

The provider gives each school dedicated login credentials for an Android application. Authorized teachers or staff members use the app to enter student information and capture live photographs with the device camera.

The photograph is associated with the corresponding student record. Details and photos are submitted together and become available in the service provider's account.

The provider can then work from those digital submissions to prepare, print and deliver ID cards. There is no need to retype the same handwritten forms into Excel or reconstruct the connection between separate photo numbers and paper records.

> Schools collect and submit the information through the Android app. The service provider manages the received records and handles ID card production and delivery.

## The digital workflow, step by step

### 1. Onboard each school

The service provider creates dedicated login credentials for each school. This gives participating schools an assigned way to submit their records.

### 2. Give authorized staff Android app access

Teachers or designated school staff log in using their school's credentials. Data collection can take place at the school without waiting for the provider to visit for each collection session.

### 3. Enter student details digitally

Staff enter student information directly into the application. The digital entry replaces handwritten collection forms and the later task of typing those forms into a spreadsheet.

### 4. Capture a live photograph

Using the device camera, staff capture the student's photograph within the application. The photo is linked to the student's record, reducing reliance on handwritten photograph numbers.

### 5. Submit the record and photograph together

Student details and the associated image are submitted digitally. They become available in the service provider's account, bringing the information needed for production into the same workflow.

### 6. Prepare, print and deliver

The service provider accesses the submitted records, prepares the ID cards, prints them and delivers the finished cards to the school. Digital collection supports the production process while the provider continues to manage the physical cards and delivery.

## What this means for an ID card printing business

**Less paperwork and repeated entry.** Digital student data collection removes the paper-to-Excel transfer from this workflow, allowing the team to spend less time processing the same information twice.

**Fewer opportunities for photo mismatches.** Capturing a photograph against its student record reduces the need to connect separate images using handwritten numbers.

**Simpler participation for schools.** Authorized staff can collect details and photographs through the Android app instead of coordinating every collection task around a provider visit.

**More organized multi-school work.** Submissions from different schools become available through the service provider's account, helping the business handle collection as a digital process.

**Earlier preparation for production.** As usable records arrive, providers can spend more time preparing cards and managing delivery, with less manual sorting and re-entry in between.

Accuracy still depends on the information entered. Reviewing names, student details and photographs before printing remains an important part of delivering the correct cards.
