An ID card project involves more than arranging a photograph and a name. Schools need accurate records, consistent layouts and a reliable way to check details before cards are printed.

A planned workflow helps the office team handle new admissions, corrections and replacement cards without recreating the same work each time.

## Prepare the information first

Collect the details your institution has decided to display. Keep the list purposeful and review whether each field belongs on a card that may be visible outside the school.

- Student or staff name
- Admission or employee identifier
- Class and section, or department
- Approved photograph
- School name and approved branding
- Validity information, where needed

Check spelling, identifiers and photograph quality before beginning bulk work. A consistent file-naming convention makes photo matching easier.

## Build one approved template

Choose readable typography and keep enough space around important information. Review a sample at its actual printed size, including the reverse side if you use one.

### Review the print requirements

Confirm the card dimensions, print margins and output format with your printing provider. A layout that looks clear on a large screen may need adjustment for a small card.

> Approve a complete sample with realistic content before generating the full batch.

## Use a clear proofing process

1. Generate a small sample from checked records.
2. Ask the responsible staff member to review names and identifiers.
3. Confirm that photographs match the correct records.
4. Check the printed proof for readability and alignment.
5. Approve the batch and keep a record of corrections.

Assign one person to coordinate final approval. This reduces conflicting instructions and makes it easier to track changes.

## Plan for replacements

Keep the original record and approved template organized so a replacement card can be generated consistently. Confirm how changes in class, department or validity will be handled during the next issue cycle.
